About Total Build

Since 1910, there has been a Smith on a job site.

Total Build is a custom home builder in Leesburg, Virginia. Brian Smith is the fourth generation of his family to build houses, and the standard he holds is not one he invented. He inherited it from people who were willing to give up a great deal to protect it.

The line

Four generations.

1910

Brian's great-grandfather begins contracting, working on major concrete projects in Washington, D.C., including several national landmarks.

1950

His grandfather takes it up, and builds custom homes in McLean under his own name: E.J. Smith Builders.

1980

His father, Chris Smith, begins contracting in Loudoun County. He is still working today.

2000

Brian begins training under his father.

2008

Brian starts Total Build.

Where the standard came from

They closed a good company on purpose.

E.J. Smith Builders had a reputation in McLean for integrity and quality, built over many successful years. When Brian's grandparents reached the end of their working life, they did not sell it, and they did not hand the name to someone else to carry.

They closed it. They were not willing to risk the family name outliving the standard behind it.

Brian was young, and it stayed with him. It is the reason this company is careful about what it puts its name on, and the reason the answer to can you do it cheaper is sometimes no.

Brian Smith

The fourth Smith.

Some of Brian's earliest memories are of riding with his father to job sites. Climbing the dirt piles from a newly excavated basement, the smell of fresh framing lumber and sawdust, watching a house take shape. He says walking onto one of his own sites still brings the same thing back.

His father pushed him toward college, so that he would not have to work as hard physically as he had. Brian took the advice and earned a degree in Communications from George Mason. Construction pulled him back anyway. What he worked out along the way was that his place was not in the field. It was guiding projects, clients and teams.

He started Total Build in 2008. He sets the standard the company builds to and stays close to the work; the day-to-day of your build belongs to your project manager.

Why custom homes

Every family is different, so every house is.

Brian will tell you that when he first came to custom homes he expected the work to get repetitive. You are handed a set of plans and you build what is on the page. He was wrong about that, and it did not take long to find out why.

A house is not really about square footage or finishes. It is about the way one particular family lives in it: where they gather, how they raise their children, which room they end up in at the end of the day. No two families answer that the same way, so no two houses we build come out the same.

Plenty of these projects start as a rough sketch on a piece of paper. Months of planning, engineering and building later, you are standing inside the framed house remembering where it began. Then a family takes the keys and moves into a home that was designed around them. That is the part of the job worth doing.

Who you will actually work with

Builders, coaches, and advocates.

That is how Brian describes the job, and it is the part people are surprised by. A custom home is not built by one person. You will have a project manager who runs your site and answers your questions, working alongside superintendents, estimators, coordinators and the administrative staff who keep a build moving.

Coaching matters as much as building. Most of our clients are doing this once. They arrive with an idea of the house and a hundred decisions they have never had to make, and our job is to make sure every one of those decisions is made with the real numbers in front of them. Advocating matters just as much. We are on your side of the table, including the parts of the job where it would be easier for us not to be.

Brian's own view of the team is that technical skill is essential and character matters just as much. If someone does not care about serving people, they are not a fit here, however good their hands are. The same test decides which subcontractors and suppliers we work with.

What we hold to

Three things, and they are not decoration.

Trust

We believe lasting relationships are built on honesty, transparency, and doing what is right even when no one is looking. We never want to profit at someone else's expense, not yours and not a subcontractor's.

Quality

There are materials we will not compromise on, because once you have seen the difference between an average product and an exceptional one it is hard to go back. We build the parts you never see to the standard of the parts you see every day.

Communication

You will know what is happening on your site, what it costs, and what comes next. Nothing arrives as a surprise at the end.

How we think about your money

We would rather you spend it well than spend it here.

We treat a client's budget the way we would treat our own. There are unlimited ways to spend money on a custom home, and our job is to help you put it where your family will actually feel it. Most people live in the kitchen, the great room and the primary suite, and that is usually where the money should go. Secondary bedrooms and bathrooms can be beautiful without consuming the budget.

Luxury is not a matter of buying the most expensive product available. It is knowing where quality genuinely matters and where it does not.

There is more creative problem-solving in this than people expect. Building in Loudoun County is expensive, but a property that already has a house on it may come with a driveway, a well, a septic system or a foundation footprint you can reuse. That infrastructure is a real share of the cost. Knowing which engineers to call and which specialists suit a particular problem is part of what you are hiring.

We are not right for everyone

And that is a real answer, not modesty.

We think it matters that you know who we are before you decide to work with us. Not every builder suits every client. If our process or the way we work does not sit right with you, another builder may honestly be the better match, and we would rather you find that out now.

One thing worth knowing: a lot of builders who advertise themselves as custom quietly discourage changes once construction starts, because changes disrupt a production schedule. We do not. Changes affect cost and schedule and we will always tell you exactly how. But we would rather adapt than hand you the house that was easiest for us to build.
